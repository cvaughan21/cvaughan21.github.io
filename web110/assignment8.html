First, I began by completing the TutorialsPoint lesson on internet security. I’ve heard of IPSec and SSL, but was not sure of the differences between them. IPsec works at the network level, and creates authenticated and confidential packets for the IP layer, whereas SSL works at the transport layer, and works on privacy, integrity, and authentication. I am definitely familiar with email phishing, and receive phishing attempts daily, which I thankfully have not made the mistake of following links or providing sensitive or personal information to. I then moved on to choosing my privacy settings in Google Chrome, which is the browser I use most often. I first looked at the alerts it had given me. While my browser was up to date with safe browsing enabled, Chrome was concerned because it detected a few reused passwords. This is something I need to be better about, as someone cracking one password would then most likely give them access to logins on other websites, giving them more access to my personal information. I then reviewed how Google Chrome protects my saved passwords and logins. One thing I found particularly interesting was how it knows not to enable autofill functions on websites just because they look similar to saved ones. This is helpful in preventing phishing attempts where you receive a link to a website, such as your bank, that looks almost identical to the real login page. However, once you enter your information, it is essentially handing off your login information to whoever created the phishing attempt, and is not just signing you into your bank account. Finally, I tested my firewall using the Gibson Research: ShieldsUP! Page. It ran a test of my router IP address, and it was successful, meaning that it did not respond to their UPnP probes. This means that my current router set up is not going to give access to UPnP from the public internet. This is a good thing, as this would give any potential hacker direct access to the internals of an exposed network.
